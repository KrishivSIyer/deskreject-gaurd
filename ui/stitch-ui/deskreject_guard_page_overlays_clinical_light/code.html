Representation Drift in Self-Supervised Agents Under Latent Dynamics

Jane Rao1            Arjun Mehta1, 2

{jane.rao, a.mehta}@example-univ.edu

1Department of Computer Science & Engineering, Example University     2Acrobat AI Labs

Abstract

Self-supervised representations frequently undergo catastrophic semantic drift when deployed in non-stationary POMDPs. In this paper, we formalize representation drift under stochastic latent transitions by bounding the Hausdorff discrepancy between consecutive temporal projection spaces. We evaluate empirical trajectories against standard contrastive benchmarks, revealing an asymptotic divergence threshold of ε > 0.28. Furthermore, we construct an adaptive projection manifold that prevents manifold collapse under partial observability.

1   Introduction

Contemporary foundation models increasingly rely on continuous state representations learned through mutual information maximization. While effective in static benchmark distributions, autonomous multi-step decision agents must maintain spatial consistency despite systemic latency.

Previous formulations assume either bounded distribution shift or immediate reward feedback. In contrast, double-blind pre-training across uncurated sensory streams introduces non-ergodic drifts. As shown in Theorem 1, the projection manifold undergoes rank degradation proportionally to sensory variance.

2   Problem Formulation

Let M = (S, A, T, R, γ) denote a continuous Markov Decision Process. An encoder f_θ : S → R^d maps observations into an embedding metric. We define drift metric Δ_t as the spectral distance between the push-forward measure and stationary target.

L_drift(θ) = E_{s_t, s_{t+1}} [ || f_θ(s_{t+1}) - W f_θ(s_t) ||_2^2 ]

Where W represents an isometric rotation matrix parameterized via orthogonal projection constraints.

To circumvent empirical collapse, common heuristics apply exponential moving averages (EMA) to target parameters. However, in Theorem 2 we illustrate that moving target encodings fail to prevent drift when noise components align with primary singular vectors.

Our contributions are three-fold:

  • A non-asymptotic lower bound on representation degeneration under unobserved Markovian confounders.
  • A projection regularizer guaranteeing manifold volume preservation with linear runtime complexity.
  • Empirical validation on DeepMind Control Suite, demonstrating a 34% reduction in semantic drift without auxiliary supervision.

3   Theoretical Bounds

Under standard Lipshitz continuity assumptions on environmental transition dynamics, the divergence between successive latent manifolds obeys the recurrence inequality:

|| Φ_t - Φ_0 ||_F ≤ Σ_{k=1}^t (1 - λ_min(K))^k · ε_latent

Where K denotes the normalized Gram matrix computed across batch observations and ε_latent is the stochastic perturbation radius.
